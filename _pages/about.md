# About Me

I am a researcher at the Institute of Atmospheric Sciences and Climate Change (ICAyCC), National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), where I work on atmospheric chemistry, air quality modeling, and numerical methods for understanding and predicting air pollution.

My research focuses on the development and evaluation of numerical models for urban and regional air quality, with particular emphasis on data assimilation, atmospheric observations, satellite measurements, and computational methods. I am also interested in combining statistical and machine-learning approaches with numerical modeling to improve air quality forecasts and reduce model bias.

My work has focused particularly on the Mexico City Metropolitan Area and the Megalopolis of Central Mexico, addressing questions related to ozone, particulate matter, atmospheric composition, emissions, pollutant transport, and meteorological conditions affecting air pollution.

I also teach and supervise students in atmospheric sciences, Earth sciences, applied mathematics, and computational science. My teaching activities include air quality modeling, numerical modeling, data assimilation, scientific computing, and computational tools for geosciences.

## Teaching & Mentoring

I teach courses at undergraduate and graduate levels at UNAM, including air quality modeling, numerical modeling, data assimilation, and computational methods.

I supervise and mentor undergraduate, master's, doctoral, and postdoctoral researchers working on atmospheric modeling, air quality, data assimilation, satellite observations, and computational methods.

I have also developed and taught specialized training activities on numerical weather and air quality modeling, including WRF and WRF-Chem.
