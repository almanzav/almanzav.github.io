# Research Projects

My research projects combine atmospheric observations, numerical modeling, data assimilation, and computational methods to investigate air quality and atmospheric composition in Mexico.

A major focus of my work is the **Megalopolis of Central Mexico**, where I study the interactions between meteorology, emissions, atmospheric chemistry, and pollutant transport. My projects also involve the use of satellite observations, ensemble and variational data assimilation, statistical methods, and machine learning.

---

## Current Projects

### PAN Transport and Ozone in the Megalopolis

**Evaluating the contribution of transported Peroxyacetyl Nitrate (PAN) from urban areas of the Megalopolis to the stability of ozone levels in Mexico City**

**Role:** Principal Investigator / Technical Lead  
**Funding:** PAPIIT–UNAM  
**Period:** 2026–present

This project investigates the contribution of transported PAN to ozone concentrations in Mexico City and its potential role in maintaining ozone levels during pollution episodes.

The study combines atmospheric modeling, chemical transport, and observational information to examine the regional transport of ozone precursors and the processes controlling ozone persistence in the Mexico City Metropolitan Area.

---

### Atmospheric Data Assimilation for the Megalopolis

**Assimilation of meteorological and atmospheric composition observations for air quality modeling**

**Role:** Principal Investigator / Technical Lead  
**Funding:** UNAM and computational research programs  
**Period:** Ongoing

This research focuses on incorporating atmospheric observations into numerical models to improve the representation of the current state of the atmosphere and air quality forecasts over the Megalopolis of Central Mexico.

The work includes both variational and ensemble-based data assimilation approaches, including the assimilation of chemical observations such as CO and NO₂.

Observations from surface monitoring networks and satellite instruments are combined with numerical models to estimate atmospheric conditions and improve air quality predictions.

---

### Air Quality Forecasting and Early Warning

**Ensemble system for early warning of pollution episodes and air quality forecasting**

**Role:** Collaborator and Component 1 Lead  
**Funding:** Comisión Ambiental de la Megalópolis (CAMe)  
**Period:** 2024–present

This collaborative project develops methods for improving air quality forecasting and the early identification of high-pollution episodes in the Megalopolis.

My contribution focuses on **assimilation of atmospheric chemistry and meteorological observations**, including the development and evaluation of data assimilation systems for chemical species.

---

### Remote Sensing of Atmospheric Composition in Mexico

**Remote sensing of atmospheric composition, variability, and trends of trace gases in Mexico**

**Role:** Collaborator  
**Funding:** PAPIIT–UNAM  
**Project:** IN 106024  
**Period:** 2024–2025

This project investigates the spatial and temporal variability of atmospheric trace gases using satellite observations and ground-based measurements.

Particular emphasis is placed on the use of satellite observations to characterize atmospheric composition and investigate the impact of fires and biomass burning on air quality in Mexico.

### COVID-19 Lockdown and Air Quality

**Impact of the COVID-19 lockdown on ozone, particulate matter, and secondary organic aerosols**

**Role:** Principal Investigator / Technical Lead  
**Funding:** UNAM–DGTIC  
**Period:** 2020–2021

This research investigated the changes in atmospheric composition associated with the reduction of human activities during the COVID-19 lockdown.

Numerical modeling was used to evaluate the sensitivity of ozone and fine particulate matter to changes in precursor emissions across the Megalopolis of Central Mexico.

The work also examined the role of regional transport and biomass burning in observed ozone concentrations during the lockdown period.

**Operational air quality forecasting and atmospheric dispersion modeling for the Gulf of Mexico**

**Role:** Collaborator

### Industrial Sources and Air Quality in Central Mexico

**Impact of external industrial sources on regional and local air quality in the Mexico City Metropolitan Area**

**Role:** Researcher  
**Period:** 2012–2014

This research investigated the contribution of industrial emissions, including emissions from elevated flares at the Tula Refinery, to SO₂ and O₃ concentrations in the Mexico City Metropolitan Area.

The study combined regional atmospheric modeling with observations to examine the transport of pollutants from industrial sources into the metropolitan area.

## Research Methods

Across these projects, I use a combination of:

- Numerical weather and air quality models
- Chemical transport modeling
- Variational data assimilation
- Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF) methods
- Surface monitoring observations
- Satellite observations
- Meteorological reanalysis products
- Statistical methods
- Machine learning
- High-performance scientific computing

These approaches are used to connect observations and numerical models, evaluate model performance, characterize emission sources, and improve the understanding and prediction of air quality.
